01 · Reflect

Know yourself

A career decision is not only about a job title. Think about your interests, strengths, values, and practical needs. DepEd career-skills materials include self-assessment and decision-making as part of career preparation.Source

◎

What interests you?

Which subjects, activities, or topics make you curious? What do you enjoy learning about outside school?

✳

What are your strengths?

What do people ask you to help with? Which skills would you like to develop further?

♡

What matters to you?

Consider creativity, stability, service, teamwork, independence, your work environment, and your practical needs.

I’m curious about… ______________________________
I’d like to use my strength in… ______________________________
I value… ______________________________
One thing I need to find out is… ______________________________

Keep an open mind. Your interests and strengths can develop over time. Use reflection to guide your research—not to limit your choices.

Try a quick activity

Find a few career areas to explore

Choose one answer in each group. You’ll get broad areas to research—not a test result or a career recommendation.

1. Which activity sounds most interesting?

2. What kind of work style appeals to you?

3. What would feel meaningful to you?

Choose one option in each group.

This activity is only a brainstorming tool. It does not measure your abilities or determine which career is right for you.

02 · Research

Explore careers

A job title alone does not tell you what a career is really like. Research the work, skills, qualifications, and settings involved. DepEd’s career-skills lesson materials recommend looking into job duties and possible education or certification requirements.Source

Learn by exploring

Talk, try, and notice what interests you

Ask questions, work on a small project, or speak with someone in a field you’re curious about. Real experiences can give you better questions to research.

Ask someone in the field

  • What does a typical workday look like?
  • What do you enjoy, and what is challenging?
  • What training or experience helped you get started?
  • What do you wish you had known earlier?
  • What advice would you give a student exploring this work?

If you interview someone, ask permission before recording or sharing their answers. Career information can change: check dates, compare sources, and confirm requirements with the relevant institution or provider.

03 · Compare

Compare pathways

There is more than one way to move toward a career. Compare possible routes and check current requirements directly with the school, training provider, or employer.

Higher education

College and other study

Explore programs that connect with your interests and goals. Check admission requirements, deadlines, costs, location, student support, and financial-assistance options. DepEd’s career-readiness materials discuss researching college opportunities and preparing applications.DepEd guide

CHED resources · UniFAST assistance informationSource

Technical-vocational

Skills training and certification

Look into the skills or qualification a program covers, its schedule and cost, and any assessment requirements. TESDA’s website provides training, scholarship, online-learning, assessment, and certification resources.TESDA

Labor-market reports · TVET statistics

Work

Entering the workforce

Research entry-level roles, requested skills, application documents, and ways to prepare for interviews. Check that job opportunities and employers are legitimate. PhilJobNet is the Philippine government’s job-matching and labor-market information portal, with career information for students and jobseekers.Source

Entrepreneurship

Starting a small venture

Before committing money, investigate who might need your product or service, what it may cost to operate, what skills or permissions you may need, and how you could test the idea on a small scale.

A few common questions

You can change your mind as you learn

Do I need to choose one career right now?

No. Start by exploring a few possibilities and choosing a useful next step. More research and experience may change what interests you.

What if I don’t know what I’m interested in?

Start with what you enjoy, what you are curious about, and what you want to learn. Try new activities, ask questions, and notice which tasks feel meaningful.

Should I choose a career only because I’m good at a subject?

Your strengths are one part of the picture. Also consider your interests, values, preferred work environment, practical needs, and the requirements of each path.

Where can I check whether information is current?

Start with official government, school, college, and training-provider websites. Confirm admission requirements, fees, deadlines, and qualifications directly with the institution.
